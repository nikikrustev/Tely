Part 1

The six ideas

Each card covers the problem, how the idea runs through the brief's five stages, the closest competitors by project ID, the riskiest technical piece, and the best-fit category.

A

TwinCast

RecommendedFits: Best technical / Azure AI (guess) · both brief tracks

We simulate a match, film it like a broadcaster, then let our own vision model watch the footage and put graded, explained overlays back on the video.

Problem
Auto-eventing from video (brief ii-d) and overlays on a real feed are the hardest asks. No team can prove its computer vision is right, because real footage has no ground truth, and real PL footage is off-limits anyway. Synthetic video gives perfect labels and stays inside the rules.
Closest competitors
126679 Match Mind AI with Auto Event (CV tracking, 2D twin, Foundry agents) is the nearest. Also 126492 The Ninety Labs, 126600 MatchSight AI, 126519 ATLAS, 126685 Kicklytics, and 126495 Floodlight for the seeded simulator.
Still different: they auto-event footage they did not make, so they can't publish accuracy. We publish pass-detection F1 and speed error against truth, draw overlays on the video itself, and release the dataset.
Five stages
  • IngestSeeded simulator emits events and 25 Hz tracking. A renderer turns it into broadcast-angle video, replayed at match clock.
  • InterpretDetector + tracker on the video → pitch positions → auto-events (pass, shot, sprint), speed, distance, ball and shot speed. Graded live against truth.
  • ExplainRules decide which moment matters (rarity, game state, what happens next). One model writes why, citing event IDs.
  • RenderTimed cue JSON drives an overlay compositor on the actual video, plus key/fill output for a vision mixer.
  • PersonaliseCasual/analyst, club, player focus, single-metric mode, language. These are filters on the same cues, not separate pipelines.
Riskiest part
Turning noisy ball tracks into reliable pass and shot events, and reading shirt numbers for player ID. Building the renderer is the second risk. Both are covered by the Day 7 gate.

Verdict. Highest ceiling and highest risk. It is planned so that the Day 7 checkpoint is already a full entry (idea B on rendered video), and the remaining days add the CV layer that makes it stand out.

B

Speed Trap

Safe fallbackFits: on-screen storytelling

Broadcast name tags that appear only when a player does something physically remarkable, and say why it mattered.

Problem
The brief names threshold-triggered speed and distance tags, plus ball and shot speed (ii-a, ii-c), but almost nobody makes them the main feature. Raw speed numbers are trivia until they are tied to a consequence.
Closest competitors
126646 Mauled by the Tigers (sprint alerts, but only a brief description), 126384 Sisyphus Engine (velocity decay), 126504 Fatigue index, 126359 Shield.
Still different: each tag is linked to what it changed (“this sprint stopped a 3v2”), and ball and shot speed sit beside player speed.
Five stages
  • IngestSynthetic events and 25 Hz tracking.
  • InterpretSpeed, acceleration, distance, shot speed, pass length; thresholds with rarity percentiles.
  • ExplainLinks the physical action to its outcome in the next 10 seconds.
  • RenderBroadcast name strap plus a 2D tactical camera.
  • PersonaliseViewer-set thresholds, followed player, km/h or mph, language.
Riskiest part
Low technical risk. The real risk is that it reads as a feature rather than a product. Simulated acceleration curves must look believable or analysts will notice.

Verdict. Solid but not memorable on its own. Its best use is as the floor of TwinCast: same metrics engine, same overlays.

C

One Number

Weak headlineFits: personalised experiences

Pick the one number you care about, and the whole broadcast is told through it.

Problem
The brief lists a single-metric viewing mode and nobody headlines it. Casual fans are buried under dashboards; one number with a story is easier to follow.
Closest competitors
126425 Control vs. Chaos and 126436 Control or Chaos (one fixed live index), 126669 Momentum Ledger, 126635 The Metronome, 126637 Football fun index.
Still different: the viewer chooses the metric, and the recap is re-cut around it.
Five stages
  • IngestSynthetic events.
  • InterpretAbout 12 metrics computed continuously, each with a baseline.
  • ExplainChange-point detection on the chosen metric; each swing gets a reason.
  • RenderA single corner bug on the feed plus a phone timeline.
  • PersonaliseThe metric choice itself, plus club and language.
Riskiest part
Product, not technology. Explanations for one metric get repetitive by the 60th minute, and judges may see it as “only personalises”, which the brief rules out.

Verdict. Too thin to headline. It works well as a viewer mode inside TwinCast, which still covers gap 4.

D

Chalk Talk

Strong secondFits: studio / on-screen storytelling

An AI telestrator: a pundit asks a question, and the answer is drawn on the paused frame.

Problem
Studio analysis still depends on an operator drawing arrows by hand. AI explanations in other entries stay as text cards and never touch the picture.
Closest competitors
126657 PlayerLens 3D (2D/3D illustrations), 126624 Tactic Replay, 126558 MatchShift, 126621 Match Microscope, 126334 PitchLens.
Still different: drawings sit on the broadcast frame in camera perspective, and they are generated from a spoken question.
Five stages
  • IngestEvents, tracking and rendered frames with the camera matrix.
  • InterpretPassing lanes, line height, overloads, pitch control.
  • ExplainThe model picks evidence and returns a small drawing language (arrows, zones, rings) plus narration.
  • RenderDrawings projected into the frame and animated.
  • PersonaliseDrawing depth for casual vs analyst, club perspective, narration language.
Riskiest part
Getting the model to choose the right evidence and produce drawings that are tactically correct. Wrong arrows on screen are worse than none.

Verdict. The best pick if you would rather target a studio category than CV. It shares the renderer with TwinCast, so a single telestration could be a TwinCast stretch goal.

E

Realism Bench

Don't headlineFits: data innovation (v)

An open, seeded synthetic football generator with a published realism report anyone can score their data against.

Problem
Every team invents its own fake data, and no one can say whether it is football-realistic.
Closest competitors
126672 Turning Point (Gillespie simulator), 126495 Floodlight, 126707 KooraVision, 126729 Fire AI.
Still different: the evaluation is the product, as a public benchmark.
Five stages
  • IngestThe generator itself.
  • InterpretMatch-level statistics and distributions.
  • ExplainReport says which checks fail and why.
  • RenderWeak: a report dashboard, not something next to a match.
  • PersonaliseWeak: club style presets only.
Riskiest part
Real data can't be used as the reference, only public aggregate ranges. Judges are unlikely to get excited by it in a 60-second video.

Verdict. It barely covers Render and Personalise. Ship it as a by-product of TwinCast (dataset plus realism report on GitHub) for data-innovation points.

F

Graphics Desk

Depends on executionFits: on-screen storytelling / broadcast

A producer's console where AI-proposed graphics are queued, approved and keyed onto the live video at the right frame.

Problem
About 60 entries mention overlays, but most stop at JSON. Few show graphics actually on air inside a production workflow.
Closest competitors
126353 MatchDesk (human producer in control), 126499 PitchPulse AI Broadcast Director, 126586/126350 Pitchside (timed overlays), 126587 Director's Cut, 126358 SignalFlow.
Still different: real compositing with safe areas and key/fill output, not just a cue list.
Five stages
  • IngestEvents and a video feed.
  • InterpretStandard metrics.
  • ExplainInsight cards with evidence.
  • RenderTemplate compositor, frame-accurate timing, key/fill.
  • PersonaliseVariants per output (studio, broadcast, stream) and per audience.
Riskiest part
Frame-accurate sync and low latency. The bigger risk is that the difference from MatchDesk and PitchPulse is hard to see in a write-up.

Verdict. Good craft, weak differentiation. Its compositor and key/fill output become TwinCast's Render stage.

Part 2 · Recommended

TwinCast in detail

Why it wins

  • It closes four of the five gaps in the intel at once: CV joined to a story layer, threshold speed and distance tags, an overlay drawn on real video, and an open dataset with a realism report. Single-metric viewing comes in as a mode, which covers the fifth.
  • Its proof is unusual. “Our auto-eventing scores X on passes against ground truth” is a sentence teams working from footage they didn't make can't easily say, and judges can check it on screen.
  • It respects the synthetic-only rule while still doing computer vision, which most CV entries can only do by using footage from somewhere else.
  • It is not a crew. Rules decide when a moment matters and one model says why. That keeps it out of the crowded multi-agent group.
  • It splits cleanly for two people: a vision track and a story track, joined by three schemas frozen on Day 1.
  • It has a floor. If CV disappoints at the Day 7 gate, you still have Speed Trap on rendered video with real overlays.

The contract between the two tracks

Everything downstream reads one timed cue format. It is the machine-readable output the brief asks for, and the same cue serves studio, broadcast and streaming.

{
  "cue_id": "c-0631",
  "match_clock": "63:12.2",
  "tc_in": "01:03:12:05",  "ttl_ms": 6000,
  "layout": "name_strap",
  "anchor": { "track_id": 17, "follow": true },
  "metric": { "name": "top_speed_kmh", "value": 35.2,
              "pct_in_match": 100 },
  "text": { "casual": "Fastest run of the match",
            "analyst": "35.2 km/h, 3rd sprint >34 since 55'" },
  "evidence": ["ev-4412", "trk-17@63:09-63:12"],
  "source": "cv",
  "truth_check": { "speed_err_kmh": 0.6, "event_match": true }
}

Azure architecture

  • Live path: replayer → CV → Event Hubs → metrics → salience → explainer → cues → compositor. YOLOv8n runs well above 25 fps on a T4-class GPU, so “live” is real, not faked.
  • One model call per selected moment, not per event. The salience rules filter first, which keeps latency and cost down.
  • Request GPU quota tonight. Azure ML and Container Apps GPU quota can take days. If it doesn't arrive, run inference as a batch and replay the results at match clock, and say so in the write-up.
  • Realism references come from public aggregate football ranges (goals per match, sprint speeds, pass counts), not PL feeds. Check this against the rules.

Risks and what to do about them

Pass and shot detection from noisy ball tracksRender a large white ball, detect passes from sudden ball-speed changes plus the nearest player, and smooth with a short window. The Day 7 gate decides.
Player identity from shirt numbersRender big back numbers and vote across each track. Fallback: identity from tracker, kit colour and position, with a visible confidence.
“CV on a rendered video is easy”Answer it in the demo: a degradation sweep (blur, compression, occlusion, camera shake) with accuracy at each level.
Simulator realismGenerate events first and tracking second. That is simpler and looks right from a broadcast camera. Publish the failing checks too; honesty scores better than overclaiming.
Azure GPU quotaRequest tonight. Fallback is batch inference replayed at match clock, stated plainly.
Scope creepNo agent crew, two languages at most, one 3-minute hero clip rather than 90 minutes of video.
